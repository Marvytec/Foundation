Why Hope Foundation exists

A foundation built in memory of _______, and in service of everyone still facing what she did.

Photo of Amara

______ was warm, determined, and never let her condition define how she showed up for the people around her. She grew up managing the routines that come with sickle cell: pain crises that could start without warning, hospital stays, and constant care over things most people never think about, like staying warm, staying hydrated and avoiding exhaustion.

What struck her family most was how often she had to explain the illness itself. To classmates, to coworkers, even to some health workers. Sickle cell is one of the most common inherited blood disorders, yet everyday understanding of it is thin, and that gap made an already hard condition harder to live with.

Amara passed away from complications related to sickle cell. Afterwards, her family kept returning to one thought: so much of her struggle could have been eased if more people understood the condition, recognised the warning signs and knew how to respond. Amara Hope Foundation was started to carry that understanding forward, so her story becomes a reason other families get support sooner.

Understanding is the first form of care.

What guides us

Our mission

To raise awareness of sickle cell disease, educate communities and schools on recognising and responding to it, and support the wellbeing and empowerment of people living with the condition and their families.

Our vision

A world where sickle cell is widely understood, where no one affected by it faces stigma or isolation, and where support reaches families early rather than in crisis.

Our approach

We combine community outreaches, plain-language education and direct support for affected families, working with partner organisations so our reach grows beyond what we could manage alone.

Meet the founder

Founder photo

The founder started Amara Hope Foundation in memory of their sister after seeing firsthand how little support and public understanding exists around sickle cell. Based partly abroad, the founder works with a small team and a growing circle of partner organisations to carry the foundation’s outreaches forward.
